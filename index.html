Ten lines we read in every bid

The full scorecard has thirty. These ten move the price the most, and each one has a published standard behind it or a question the builder has to answer in writing.

Soils report
The court builders' trade association recommends a soils investigation because it cuts change orders. If no bid includes one, ask who pays when the excavator hits bad ground.
The slab
Asphalt, reinforced concrete and post-tensioned concrete are three different courts at three different prices. A bid that says "concrete court" with no thickness hasn't told you which one you're buying.
Cure and moisture
Laykold's published spec gives new concrete at least 30 days before coating, with a moisture test first. A schedule that coats sooner needs a primer line in the price.
Coats
The same spec calls for one or two coats of resurfacer, two coats of color, then lines. Count the coats in each bid.
Flat and drained
No more than a quarter inch of deviation under a 10 foot straightedge. Ask every builder for the slope as a number and the test they run before they coat.
Power
Someone has to bring electricity from the meter to the courts. Check each bid for who, how far and how much.
Permits and stamped drawings
Who pulls the permit and who pays the engineer. If the answer is "by owner," it comes out of your budget.
Warranty
Years, what it covers, what voids it, and the day it starts. All four in writing.
Padel glass and height
The federation's rules call for a 10 by 20 meter court with at least 6 meters of clear height. The UK governing body's guidance calls for 12 mm tempered glass outdoors.
Noise
If homes are close, ask for the barrier's tested rating in writing, or an acoustic study.

Sources: ASBA-sponsored bid guidance (SportsField Management, September 2025). Laykold Advantage System Specification (2023). FIP Rules of Padel (December 2025). LTA padel court construction guidance (2025).

What we do

The bid review is the fastest way to use us. The rest runs from the first look at a site to a club that is already open.

Review the bids

Send us every bid as the builder sent it. You get back a short memo your board can read and a report that stands behind it.

  • Every bid on the same scope, line by line
  • Each gap priced, so the totals can be compared
  • Written questions for each builder, and their answers
  • A two-page memo your board can vote from
  • A walk-through with your committee

Before you build

  • Feasibility

    Should you build, where, and how many courts. We read the site, the lease, the market around it and the numbers. You get a yes or a no, with the reasons.

  • Design and budget

    Court count, layout, which way the courts face, sound, lighting, and where people sit. Renderings your members can react to and a budget with every line priced.

  • Business plan

    Membership and court pricing, programming, staffing, and a pro forma a lender or a board can read.

When you buy

  • Run the bid

    No bids yet. We write the scope, invite the builders and level what comes back. Same for the lighting, the booking software and the rest of what you have to buy.

  • Run the job

    We stay through construction as your representative. We read the pay applications and the change orders, stand at the pour and walk the punch list.

  • Build it

    Court Collective Construction is our building arm, now being set up. One contract, one warranty, and you see the trade bids. Until it is licensed in your state, a licensed builder holds the contract and we represent you.

Once you're open

  • Opening

    Hiring and training, the first programming calendar, the brand and the website, and the weeks before the doors open.

  • Club audit

    For a club that is already running. We go through the books, payroll, staffing, programming, pricing and court use, and hand you the list of what to change first.

  • Leases, insurance and utilities

    Three contracts that decide whether a club makes money. Rene Prats reads yours the way he reads his own.

We never bid against a builder whose bid we've read. Our fee is fixed before you sign and is the same whichever builder wins. When our building arm holds a contract, the builder you chose still does the work.

We haven't decided whether to build. Is it too early?

No. Start with feasibility. You get a yes or a no before you spend on drawings.

A club audit. We go through the books, staffing, programming and pricing, then the lease, the insurance and the utility bills.
